🌟 More Vocabulary Practice

📘 Mini lesson 1: Synonyms & antonyms

A synonym has a similar meaning. An antonym has the opposite meaning.

Example: happy and joyful are synonyms. Happy and sad are antonyms.

Try it: Which word is a synonym for joyful?

📘 Mini lesson 2: Word families

Prefixes go at the beginning of a word. Suffixes go at the end. They can change a word's meaning or form.

Example: kind + un- = unkind. Help + -ful = helpful.

Try it: What does un- do in the word unkind?

📘 Mini lesson 3: Context clues

Context clues are words around an unfamiliar word that help you understand its meaning.

Example: “The desert was arid, with no rain and very little water.” Arid means dry.

Try it: What does arid mean in the example?

📘 Mini lesson 4: Use new words

A strong sentence uses a word in a way that makes its meaning clear. Remember a capital letter at the start and an ending mark.

Example: “My generous friend shared her crayons with me.”

Try it: Which sentence shows someone being generous?
