Which connection do you need?

Choose based on who is connecting and where the learning group is based. Each person must use their own account. Never share passwords, private student details or classroom codes publicly.

Family and guardian access

Family Network

Connect a parent or legal guardian to their own child and, where appropriate, the child's approved teacher. This is for family supervision, whether the family lives in one country or moves between countries.

Access rule: a parent should see only the child or children linked to that parent account.

One country or local community

Local School Network

Connect a teacher, learners and their families in the same country or local school community. A parent connection remains a Family Network link as well.

Access rule: teachers should see only learners approved for their own classrooms.

Cross-border collaboration

International Network

Connect approved schools, teachers and learners in different countries — for example, a classroom in the UAE collaborating with a classroom in India or Pakistan.

Access rule: cross-border participation needs explicit approval, guardian consent where required, and country-aware access controls.

How secure access should work

  1. Each adult and learner uses an individual account. Do not share logins.
  2. A teacher creates and owns a classroom; joining requests remain pending until approved.
  3. A parent or legal guardian gives consent through their own account before a child participates where consent is required.
  4. Only approved classroom members should see classroom information; parents should see only their linked children.
  5. International collaboration must not bypass the same approval and privacy checks. Country boundaries must be enforced by the database, not just by this screen.

Important: current implementation status

This screen explains the three intended connection types. The existing classroom flow has sign-in, teacher-owned classroom and membership/guardian-consent checks. However, Local and International are not yet separate database network types, and country-based access boundaries have not yet been implemented and tested in Supabase.

Until that backend work is complete, do not use this screen as proof that cross-country access is secured. The next engineering step is to add a network type and verified country data to the classroom records, then enforce the permitted membership rules with Supabase Row Level Security (RLS) and test both allowed and denied access.
